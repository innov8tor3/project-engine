Source URL Scoring Protocol

A structured evaluation algorithm for assessing websites, apps, and projects against nine core dimensions of market potential, founder strength, product quality, and ecosystem engagement.

v1.0 · Glass Protocol
1

TAM / SAM / SOM Market Evaluations

▼

Assess whether the source provides (or enables inference of) Total Addressable Market, Serviceable Addressable Market, and Serviceable Obtainable Market estimates grounded in reputable analyst projections.

  • Presence of explicit TAM/SAM/SOM figures with sources
  • Citation of leading practitioners (McKinsey, Gartner, IDC, CB Insights, PitchBook, etc.)
  • Logical derivation of SOM from SAM and realistic capture assumptions
  • Currency and geographic relevance of the data
Score 0–10: 0 = no market sizing · 3–4 = vague or unsourced claims · 6–7 = solid secondary data · 9–10 = clear, sourced, multi-layer TAM/SAM/SOM with credible projections
2

Founder Status Multiplier (Grok / Public Repo Signals)

▼

Evaluate founder / team track record using publicly observable signals (GitHub/GitLab activity, prior exits, domain expertise visibility, long-term contribution history). Treat this as a multiplier or weighted indicator of execution probability.

  • OG / Proven: Multiple prior successes, deep domain history, strong public technical footprint
  • Intermediate: Relevant experience, some shipped products, moderate visibility
  • Beginner / First-time: Limited public track record
Score 0–10 (or multiplier 0.7×–1.3×): Beginner ≈ 3–5 · Intermediate ≈ 6–8 · OG / serial ≈ 9–10. Apply as a soft multiplier on the overall total if desired.
3

Authenticity & Originality

▼

Judge how genuine and distinctive the offering appears. Look for unique insights, non-templated positioning, original research or data, and avoidance of generic marketing language.

  • Evidence of original thinking vs. copy-paste industry tropes
  • Transparency about limitations and real user stories
  • Distinctive voice, design, or technical approach
Score 0–10: High scores require clear differentiation and credible, non-hype communication.
4

Depth of Subject Knowledge

▼

Measure demonstrated expertise in the domain. Depth is evidenced by technical documentation quality, accurate use of domain terminology, nuanced discussion of edge cases, and references to primary sources or standards.

  • Quality of whitepapers, docs, or blog content
  • Handling of complexity and edge conditions
  • Alignment with established domain frameworks
Score 0–10: Surface-level marketing = low · Practitioner-level insight with supporting evidence = high.
5

Gamification of UX

▼

Evaluate the presence and sophistication of game-design elements that increase engagement without feeling manipulative: progress systems, meaningful rewards, challenges, feedback loops, and social proof mechanics that feel native to the product.

  • Progression, badges, levels, or streaks that serve a real purpose
  • Delightful micro-interactions and feedback
  • Balance between motivation and user autonomy
Score 0–10: None or pure dark-pattern gamification = low · Thoughtful, value-aligned systems = high.
6

Security Provision in App / Product

▼

Assess visible and documented security posture: authentication options, data protection claims, compliance mentions (SOC 2, GDPR, ISO, etc.), transparent security pages, bug-bounty or responsible-disclosure programs, and architectural signals of care.

  • Auth methods, encryption statements, privacy policy quality
  • Public security documentation or trust center
  • History of responsible vulnerability handling (if observable)
Score 0–10: Minimal or absent signals = low · Comprehensive, verifiable security practices = high.
7

Community Building

▼

Gauge the health and intentionality of the community around the project: active forums, Discord/Slack/Telegram presence, user-generated content, events, contribution pathways, and evidence of two-way engagement.

  • Activity levels and quality of discussion
  • Official community channels and moderation quality
  • Opportunities for users to contribute or co-create
Score 0–10: No community or ghost town = low · Vibrant, well-tended community with clear ownership = high.
8

Competitions Entered & Results

▼

Look for participation in accelerators, hackathons, startup competitions, awards, or ranked lists, and note placement or recognition received.

  • Named competitions, cohorts, or award programs
  • Finalist / winner / notable mention status
  • Recency and relevance of the competitions
Score 0–10: No evidence = 0–2 · Participation = mid · Strong placements or multiple recognitions = high.
9

Partnerships with Sector Bodies

▼

Evaluate formal or publicly acknowledged partnerships, memberships, or collaborations with industry associations, standards bodies, regulators, universities, or established sector organisations.

  • Named partners and nature of the relationship
  • Joint initiatives, certifications, or co-developed assets
  • Strategic vs. purely promotional partnerships
Score 0–10: None = low · Credible, active partnerships with recognised bodies = high.

Total Score

Sum of Categories 1–9 (each 0–10) → Maximum 90

Optional: apply Founder Status as a multiplier (0.7×–1.3×) to the total.
Interpret bands roughly as: 0–30 emerging · 31–55 solid · 56–75 strong · 76–90 exceptional.

How to Use This Protocol

1

Copy the URL of this algorithm / protocol page.

2

Copy the submission website (or app / project) URL you wish to evaluate.

3

Ask Grok (or another capable AI) to evaluate the source URL against this algorithm URL. Request individual scores for each of the nine categories plus a total score, with brief justifications.

4

Review the returned scores. Iterate or request deeper evidence if any category appears under-supported.
